- Compression blocking and keyframes in best-frame picking: video codecs leave a grid of block edges (8 to 16 px) that the focus score read as detail, so a blocky frame could beat a cleaner one. `BlockGrid` finds the grid by looking for periodic step energy across columns and rows (the method behind FFmpeg's `blockdetect`, after Muijs and Kirenko; periods 4 to 32, works on cropped video because the phase is measured, not assumed). `BlockAware` scores the frame again with the pixels that straddle those edges left out (`FocusScore.detail(luma, w, h, blocks)`) and turns the ratio into a factor from 0 to 1 that fades in between a grid strength of 0.04 and 0.10, so a frame with no grid keeps factor 1. The factor is measured during extraction on a centred crop of at most 960x540 of the full frame (`BlockProbe`, called from `SampleWriter` and `FfmpegFrameProcessor`) and stored in `projects/<id>/quality/blocks.tsv`, one `index<TAB>permille` line per blocky frame, with no database change (`BlockLedger`); `FrameSharpnessAnalyzer.scoreProject` multiplies the thumbnail score by it, so best frames, the dataset export and smart keyframes all use it. Frames extracted before this change have no entry and keep factor 1. Keyframes are not preferred in general: `KeyframeTie` picks a keyframe only when its score is within 1% of the best frame of the window (`SharpestPicker`, `DatasetSelector`; `ScoredFrame.isKeyframe`). Measured on 288 frames of natural images in five codecs, the share of picks that are close to the best frame rose from 88% to 91%, and in MPEG-4 from 75% to 90%; a fixed keyframe preference did not improve the result consistently (x264 keyframes are slightly better, MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 keyframes are not). The skip-blurry filter of extraction uses the same factor (`ExtractQualityGate`: the history and the new frame are both compared on the corrected scale, and a resumed run seeds its history from the ledger through `GateResume`); it applies to video only (`ExtractRequest.newGate(blockAware)`, animated images pass false) and its effect on the number of skipped frames was not measured separately. Extraction by keyframes needs no change: every frame it keeps is a keyframe, so the tie rule has nothing to decide, while the factor applies as usual. Tests: `BlockGridTest`, `BlockAwareTest`, `KeyframeTieTest`, `BlockLedgerTest`, `ExtractQualityGateBlockTest`.

- Tag detection, cars tagged as people, fashion and "Full body": a video with only a car got Vehicles, People & faces, Fashion & accessories and Body tags. Three causes. (1) The pose step counted landmarks with `inFrameLikelihood >= 0.7` as a person, but ML Kit documents that value as the chance that the point is inside the picture, not that a person exists, and the pose model returns a skeleton for any prominent subject; a car gave 20+ "confident" landmarks. A skeleton now counts only at 0.80 or more and when shoulders and a hip form a human-shaped torso (`PoseEvidence.humanShaped`, `TagRules.poseTags`), and `SceneReasoner` keeps the Body tag only when a face or a person label agrees, or when the skeleton is very complete (20+ landmarks) and no car is on the frame. (2) That false body forced an `l:Person` label, which filled the People & faces family; it now follows the confirmed body only. (3) The ML Kit object detector has five categories (fashion, food, home goods, places, plants) and no vehicle, so it puts cars into one of them, and `keepObject` always kept `o:fashion_good`. Fashion is now kept only next to a confirmed person (confidence 0.6 or more), and fashion or home-good guesses on a frame with a confident car label need 0.9. `TagFamilies.withFamilies` also skips the People and Fashion families on a car frame with no person evidence, so words like Ring, Strap, Cap or Muscle cannot make a car a person. `PeopleFilter` (People only) uses the same human-shaped check, with 20 landmarks when there is no face. `FrameTagger.VERSION` is 4, so saved frames are scanned again once. Tests: `PoseEvidenceTest`, `PoseFixtures`, and new cases in `SceneReasonerTest`, `TagRulesTest`, `TagFamiliesTest`, `PeopleRuleTest`, `TagVersionTest`.

- Tag detection looks closer, so small faces, smiles and cars in a thumbnail are found: ML Kit needs about 100x100 px per face (Google's face-detection guidance), and every detector shrinks its input, so a face or car that is small inside a frame was missed or misread, and the smile / eyes values of small faces were ignored. Detection now works the way sliced inference (SAHI) does for small objects. (1) Faces (`FaceScanner`): whole frame, then four overlapping enlarged tiles, then every small or tile-only face is cut out with a margin, enlarged (read from the full-size picture with `BitmapRegionDecoder` when the frame has one) and checked again; that close-up gives a trustworthy smile and eye reading (`FaceMark.zoomed`), and a face that only a tile saw is kept only when the close-up confirms it, boxes cut by a tile border or with an impossible shape are dropped, and duplicates are merged. The analysis side of `assessFrame` follows `AnalysisSource.side` (1024 px, 768 on weak devices) instead of a fixed 768. (2) Labels and objects (`FrameTagger`): the same tiles are labelled and scanned for objects; a label seen only on a tile has to be confident (0.78) or be seen by two tiles, while one the whole frame already saw gets a small bonus (`SmartZoom.vote`); small objects (down from 64 px to 28 px) are cut out, enlarged and labelled again, with a higher floor when the enlargement is large, so a wrong guess on a blown-up speck is not kept. Tiles are skipped on devices that report reduced analysis. (3) Extraction by content (`ContentFilter`): sampled at 768 px (was 512), faces and smiles go through `FaceScanner.detect`, and labels are also checked on tiles in Loose and Balanced modes (Strict stays whole-frame only). `FrameTagger.VERSION` is 3, so saved frames are scanned again once. Crowds: up to 12 faces get the close-up (tile-only ones first), and a tile-only face past that limit is kept when the tile already saw it at 100 px or more instead of being dropped. If the region decoder cannot read the full picture, it is decoded once at up to 2048 px and the windows are cut from that copy (`FullResRegions`). A label that only a tile saw now gets the boxes of the objects found in that tile (not on a person), so the animal-near-person rule uses a real position instead of demanding 0.92; the tile pass runs once and feeds both the label and object steps (`zoomPass`, `localise`). The geometry and the voting rules are plain Kotlin (`SmartZoom`) with tests in `SmartZoomTest`; `FaceQualityTest` covers zoomed faces.
